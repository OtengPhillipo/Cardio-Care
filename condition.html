Understanding Hypertension

Hypertension, or high blood pressure, is a long-term condition where the force of blood against the artery walls stays too high. It often develops without obvious symptoms, so many people do not know they have it until it is checked.

Why It Matters

High blood pressure makes the heart work harder and can damage blood vessels over time. If it is not controlled, it raises the risk of heart attack, stroke, kidney disease, and other serious health problems.

Common Signs

Most people with hypertension feel normal, which is why it is often called a "silent condition." When blood pressure becomes very high, some people may experience headaches, dizziness, chest pain, blurred vision, or shortness of breath, but these are not always present. Other severe symptoms include:

  • Nausea and vomiting
  • Anxiety or confusion
  • Buzzing in the ears
  • Nosebleeds
  • Abnormal heart rhythm

Causes and Risk Factors

Hypertension can be influenced by family history, age, unhealthy diet, excessive salt intake, lack of exercise, obesity, smoking, alcohol use, stress, and other medical conditions. Some medicines and underlying health problems can also contribute.

Diagnosis

Blood pressure is measured with a cuff, and readings are usually written as two numbers, such as 120/80 mm Hg. Many guidelines define hypertension as readings that are consistently 130/80 mm Hg or higher, while the World Health Organization (WHO) uses 140/90 mm Hg or higher in its fact sheet.

Treatment & Living with Hypertension

Treatment usually includes lifestyle changes and, when needed, medication. Common lifestyle steps are eating a healthy diet, reducing salt, exercising regularly, maintaining a healthy weight, limiting alcohol, quitting smoking, managing stress, and taking prescribed medicines correctly.

Because hypertension is often silent, regular blood pressure checks are important even when you feel well. Following treatment consistently helps prevent complications and keeps blood pressure under better control over time.
